# ai-cold-email-writer: does what the emails say match the lead data?

## Numbers first
| | Dev baseline (16 leads × 2) | Dev with fix (16 × 3) | Held-out baseline (11 × 5) | Held-out with fix (11 × 5) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Emails with an unsupported or wrong claim, or sent when they shouldn't be | 13/32 (41%, CI 26–58%) | 0/48 (0%, CI 0–7%) | 12/55 (22%, CI 13–34%) | 0/55 (0%, CI 0–7%) |
| …of which `emails.py check` flagged | 0 | – | 0 | – |
| Clean leads skipped by mistake | 0 | 0 | 0 | 0 |

- **Held-out change:** 4 leads got better and 0 got worse. Lead-bootstrap 95% CI on the fail-rate change: −47 to −2 percentage points. McNemar on leads: p = 0.125. There are only 11 held-out leads, so the per-lead test can't reach significance even though every changed lead improved.
- **Correction after I read the emails myself:** 2 of the 12 held-out baseline failures are checker false alarms (see below). The honest baseline is about 10/55 (18%). On dev, 2 of the 13 baseline failures were judge false alarms (honest baseline about 11/32).
- **Budget:** about 190 writer calls used of about 600.

## What's wrong, by root cause
1. **A bug in `facts()`, not the model (unknown_tenure: 4/4 dev, 5/5 held-out).** A missing "Years/Months in Position" becomes 0 months, so `months < 6` makes `new_in_role = true`. The writer is told the lead is new and dutifully writes "Congrats on the new role". Rewording the prompt can't fix this; the input itself is false.
2. **A gap in the pipeline (suppressed: 4/4 dev, 5/5 held-out).** The writer never sees the Instantly block list, so it writes emails to people who unsubscribed. That is a compliance risk, not a writing-quality issue.
3. **The model misattributes numbers (distractor_number: 2–3 of 4 dev baseline emails were real failures).** Results from a past employer in the summary ("grew a team 4→40", "300% revenue") get presented as the lead's current team or result, often in the subject line. The number really is in the data, so number-matching checks pass it.
4. **Traps that held in the baseline anyway:** negative growth, funding hints, not hiring, missing sales team size, and link-site domains (the model skipped these 2/2 on its own). On these leads the baseline model was mostly fine, but nothing enforced it.

## The fix (one change, general, names no lead)
- **Guards:**
  - `tenure_unknown`: missing tenure stays null.
  - `suppression_filter`: drop leads on the block list before writing.
  - `link_site_skip`: deterministic skip, enforcing a rule SKILL.md already has.
- **Prompt rules:** every claim must trace back to a fact field. Results from a past employer or client must be named as such or left out, in the subject too. Growth words only when growth is above 0. "New role" only when tenure is known and under 6 months or the data flags a new position. No sales team size unless one is given. No funding claims unless a round is stated. No hiring claims unless stated. Don't present guesses about the lead's habits as facts. Skip when no real hook remains.

## What the checkers miss
- **`emails.py check`:** caught 0/10 bad canaries and 0 of the 25 real failures. It checks form only.
- **Rules:** caught 8/10 canaries. They miss misattribution (the right number from the wrong company). One false alarm on held-out: "that scale" read as growth when it referred to team size.
- **LLM judge:** caught 10/10 canaries with 0/6 false alarms, but the CI on 16 canaries is wide (false-alarm rate could be up to 39%). On real emails it flagged hedged guesses ("you might be rebuilding…", "building prospect lists") as unsupported: 3 of its 15 flags across the two baselines were false alarms, by my reading. Use both checkers, and have a human read anything flagged by the judge alone.

## What I'm not sure about
- **Most of the held-out gain comes from the two guards**, which are deterministic. On held-out, the model-level traps (distractor, funding, hiring) did not fail even in the baseline (0/5 each). So the held-out data doesn't prove the attribution rules work; that evidence is dev only (from 2–3/4 failures to 0/6), on leads I looked at while writing the rules.
- **Small samples:** 1–2 held-out leads per family. "0/55" has an upper bound of about 7%, and that is not proof of zero.
- **I checked truthfulness, not reply rates.** The stricter rules may make emails blander. Worth a reply-rate A/B.
- **Recommendation:** merge the `facts()` tenure fix and the block-list filter now; both are plain bugs. Keep the prompt rules, and add a check for claims attributed to the wrong company. Neither the current check nor the rules can catch those.
